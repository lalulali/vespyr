## 1. Course Overview & Description

**Course Title:** Jobs-to-Be-Done for Cross-Functional Teams — From Theory to Roadmap Commit

**Style Mode:** Intermediate

**Audience:** Product managers, designers, engineers, and researchers already building products who want an operational command of JTBD (not a survey course for absolute beginners).

**Summary:** Over four weeks the course takes a cross-functional team from "we sort of know what a job is" to "we run switch interviews, generate Opportunity-ranked outcomes, and commit a release around the top three jobs". Each module pairs a conceptual foundation with a hands-on team lab that produces a validated artifact shared in subsequent modules.

**Total Duration:** 4 weeks · 6 hours/week · 24 hours total

---

## 2. Prerequisites & Learning Expectations

**Mandatory pre-work:**

- 1+ year of contribution to a product development team (PM, design, engineering, research, or analytics).
- Currently assigned to a real product surface with discoverable users (internal tool, B2B SaaS feature, or consumer surface).
- Comfort applying a basic 5-question interview protocol.
- Chrome/Zoom and a willingness to record interviews.

**Material expectations:**

- Each learner will produce 1 switch interview recorded transcript per week starting Week 2.
- Each learner will contribute one job statement drafted from a real session per week starting Week 2.
- All written artifacts (transcripts, jobs, outcome scores) are uploaded to the team's research repository; reviewed at the next week's class.

## 4. Detailed Module Objectives (Bloom-Tagged)

### M1 — Foundations: What a Job Really Is

- **Objective 1:** `[REMEMBER]` Recall the Christensen milkshake study and identify the two distinct jobs the product was hired for.
- **Objective 2:** `[UNDERSTAND]` Define a Job as the functional, emotional, and social progress sought in a circumstance.
- **Objective 3:** `[ANALYZE]` Contrast persona-led segmentation with job-led segmentation and identify where each is appropriate.
- **Objective 4:** `[UNDERSTAND]` Explain why two personas can hire the same product for the same job, and one persona can hire different products for different jobs.

### M2 — Core Mechanics: Job Statements & Forces of Progress

- **Objective 1:** `[APPLY]` Write job statements in Ulwick form (`When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]`) and Bedford form (`Help me [verb] [object] [context]`) correctly.
- **Objective 2:** `[UNDERSTAND]` Enumerate the four Forces of Progress (Push, Pull, Anxiety, Inertia) and explain why Anxiety + Inertia usually outweigh Push + Pull.
- **Objective 3:** `[ANALYZE]` Decompose a single observed switch event into the forces active in each timeline phase.
- **Objective 4:** `[UNDERSTAND]` Trace the JTBD timeline from first thought through ongoing use, and locate where onboarding UX must reduce Anxiety/Inertia vs. marketing must amplify Pull.

### M3 — Operationalization: Switch Interviews & Job Mining

- **Objective 1:** `[APPLY]` Conduct a switch interview that walks backward from the switch event to first thought with minimum speculation.
- **Objective 2:** `[APPLY]` Code each timeline event into the active force (Push/Pull/Anxiety/Inertia) with a one-line trigger and supporting quote.
- **Objective 3:** `[CREATE]` Aggregate five switch interview worksheets into candidate job statements that pass the verb-object-context clarifier test.
- **Objective 4:** `[APPLY]` Generate outcome statements per Ulwick ODI templates (minimize-time, minimize-likelihood, maximize-extent).
- **Objective 5:** `[EVALUATE]` Compute Opportunity Scores for twenty outcomes and rank-identify the top five for roadmap commitment.

### M4 — Integration: From Jobs to Product Decisions

- **Objective 1:** `[APPLY]` Translate the top five Opportunity-ranked outcomes into a release-level commitment subset (3–5 items).
- **Objective 2:** `[CREATE]` Draft a design brief for each top job with target circumstance, current force balance, proposed force shift, and success metric.
- **Objective 3:** `[APPLY]` Map the Pull force to external marketing messaging and the Anxiety/Inertia forces to onboarding UX interventions.
- **Objective 4:** `[EVALUATE]` Distinguish leading (job-completion) from lagging (engagement) indicators and select a North-Star metric for a current product.
- **Objective 5:** `[EVALUATE]` Resolve job-surface collisions by separable audience, separable circumstance, or explicit deprioritization recorded in `artifacts/memory/active-decisions.md`.

### M5 — Pitfalls & Anti-Patterns

- **Objective 1:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose aspirational jobs ("be more productive") from symptom and prescribe the verb-object-context fix.
- **Objective 2:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose solution-shaped jobs ("use a mobile app") and prescribe the underlying functional-progress fix.
- **Objective 3:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose persona-driven jobs and prescribe re-clustering by circumstance with persona metadata retained for messaging only.
- **Objective 4:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose single-interview generalization (n=1 pivot); prescribe n=5 minimum threshold.
- **Objective 5:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose ignoring Anxiety/Inertia in onboarding; prescribe instrumented first-week uninstall tracking.

## 5. Assessment & Evaluation Strategy

Progress is verified across three assessment cohorts following Bloom's hierarchy from REMEMBER → EVALUATE:

### 5.1 Formative Quizzes (Weekly)

Each synchronous session begins with a 10-question quiz probing the previous module's Bloom range:

| Week | Question type | Sample Bloom | Pass threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 → M2 | Recall + Comprehension multiple choice + short answer | REMEMBER, UNDERSTAND | 80% |
| M2 → M3 | Application multiple choice (job-statement validity tests) | APPLY | 75% |
| M3 → M4 | Forces-coding short scenario | APPLY, ANALYZE | 75% |
| M4 → M5 | Integration essay | ANALYZE, EVALUATE | 70% |

### 5.2 Hands-On Lab Projects (Weekly)

| Module | Lab artifact | Pass criteria |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Persona-vs-Job comparison table for the learner's current product | All three job dimensions specified; two personas mapped to two distinct jobs. |
| M2 | One Ulwick and one Bedford job statement each for the learner's product, all passing the 3-component test | Both statements pass tests. |
| M3 | Two recorded switch interviews with completed Forces worksheets | Backward-walk structure applied; each event coded to one force with quote. |
| M4 | Three completed design briefs for top-three Opportunity-ranked jobs | All five rows present; success metric is job-completion not engagement. |
| M5 | Diagnostic recital — learner presents one pitfall diagnosis with prescription for an existing roadmap item | Symptom and fix documented; reviewer passes. |

### 5.3 Capstone Exercise (Final)

A two-week capstone following the final synchronous session:

1. Team conducts **five additional switch interviews** with their current product's non-switchers (to surface Anxiety/Inertia gaps).
2. Team computes Opportunity Scores for **ten outcomes** and commits a **release-level subset of three jobs** with design briefs.
3. Team writes a **post-integration diagnostic note** identifying which of the M5 pitfalls they observed internally and the prescribed mitigations, committed to the team's `lessons-learned.md`.

**Capstone sign-off criteria:**

- [ ] Interview worksheet artifact committed (n ≥ 5).
- [ ] Opportunity-ranked outcome table committed.
- [ ] Three design briefs committed with all five rows.
- [ ] Post-integration diagnostic note committed.
- [ ] Reviewed and signed off by course facilitator.
