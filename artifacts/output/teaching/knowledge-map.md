## Module Breakdown

### Module 1: Foundations — What a "Job" Really Is

- **Module Objective:** `[UNDERSTAND]` Explain the JTBD mindset shift from attributes-of-users to progress-sought-by-users, and articulate why this reframing changes downstream product decisions.
- **Sub-Concepts:**
  1. **Definition of a Job**: `[UNDERSTAND]` A Job is the progress a person is trying to make in a particular circumstance — not a task, not a feature request, not a "need".
  2. **Origin & lineage**: `[REMEMBER]` Clayton Christensen's milkshake research (1990s) framed demand around hiring a product to do a job. Tony Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) formalized it commercially [1].
  3. **Functional vs. Emotional vs. Social jobs**: `[UNDERSTAND]` Every job carries three nested dimensions — what the user is functionally accomplishing, how they feel about it, and how they perceive others will judge it.
  4. **Why JTBD ≠ personas**: `[ANALYZE]` Personas describe *who*; jobs describe *why*. Two different personas can hire the same product for the same job; one persona can hire different products for different jobs.
- **If Nothing Else, Remember This:** People don't buy products — they *hire* them to make progress in a specific circumstance. The product is the means, not the end.

### Module 2: Core Mechanics — Job Statements & Forces of Progress

- **Module Objective:** `[APPLY]` Write well-formed job statements using the standard grammatical template, and decompose any product interaction into the four Forces of Progress.
- **Sub-Concepts:**
  1. **Job statement template**: `[APPLY]` `Verb + object + (clarifying context)`. Direction: `When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].` (Ulwick form) or `Help me [verb][object] [context]` (Bedford form).
  2. **Main job vs. related jobs**: `[ANALYZE]` A user typically has one main job and 3–8 related jobs. Catalog the whole system before ranking.
  3. **Forces of Progress (Bob Moesta)**: `[UNDERSTAND]` Four forces govern switching behavior:
     - *Push* of the current situation (what's wrong today)
     - *Pull* of the new solution (what the future promises)
     - *Anxiety* of the new solution (uncertainty about switching)
     - *Inertia* of the current situation (habit / switching cost)
  4. ** JTBD timeline**: `[UNDERSTAND]` First thought → passive looking → active looking → deciding → onboarding → ongoing use. Time-to-value is governed by the push/pull/anxiety/inertia balance.
- **If Nothing Else, Remember This:** A job statement is a precise grammatical object — verb, object, contextual clarifier — not a vague aspiration. Switching is a four-force tug-of-war, and anxiety+inertia usually outweigh push+pull.

### Module 3: Operationalization — Switch Interviews & Job Mining

- **Module Objective:** `[APPLY]` Conduct a switch interview from the timeline backward, extract job statements from transcripts, and validate candidate jobs against observable behavior.
- **Sub-Concepts:**
  1. **Switch interview protocol**: `[APPLY]` Start at the moment of purchase/switch, walk *backward* in time to first thought. Avoid speculation; anchor to specific dates and events.
  2. **Forces extraction**: `[APPLY]` For each timeline event, code which of the 4 forces was active and what triggered it (push event, pull event, anxiety spike, inertia barrier).
  3. **Job statement synthesis**: `[CREATE]` Cluster narrative observations into candidate job statements; test each for verb-object-context completeness.
  4. **Outcome statements (Ulwick ODI)**: `[APPLY]` Each job has 10–50 desired outcomes phrased as `minimize the time it takes to…` / `minimize the likelihood of…` / `maximize the extent to which…`.
  5. **Opportunity algorithm scoring**: `[EVALUATE]` Score each outcome on Importance and Satisfaction (1–10), compute Opportunity Score = Importance + max(Importance − Satisfaction, 0). Rank from high to low.
- **If Nothing Else, Remember This:** Walk interviews backward from the switch event — forward recall produces rationalization; backward recall produces causation. Opportunity lives where Importance is high and Satisfaction is low.

### Module 4: Integration — From Jobs to Product Decisions

- **Module Objective:** `[ANALYZE]` Map ranked jobs/outcomes to roadmap, design, and messaging decisions, and justify the trade-offs that arise when competing jobs share the same surface.
- **Sub-Concepts:**
  1. **Roadmap prioritization**: `[APPLY]` Plot ranked Opportunity Scores; commit to the top 3–5 outcomes per release. Defer features tied to low-Opportunity jobs.
  2. **Design brief generation**: `[CREATE]` Translate each top job into a design brief: target circumstance, current force-balance, proposed force-shift, success metric.
  3. **Messaging & positioning**: `[APPLY]` External messaging speaks to the *pull* force; onboarding UX neutralizes the *anxiety* and *inertia* forces.
  4. **Metric selection**: `[EVALUATE]` Choose North-Star metrics that reflect hired-for progress, not engagement. Usage is a lagging indicator; job completion is the leading indicator.
  5. **Conflict resolution across jobs**: `[EVALUATE]` When two top jobs crowd the same surface, decide via (a) separable audience, (b) separable circumstance, or (c) explicit deprioritization — never via silent compromise.
- **If Nothing Else, Remember This:** The job is the unit of roadmap strategy. Engagement metrics that don't reflect job completion are vanity metrics.

### Module 5: Pitfalls & Anti-Patterns

- **Module Objective:** `[EVALUATE]` Diagnose common JTBD implementation failures and prescribe corrections for each failure mode.
- **Sub-Concepts:**
  1. **Aspirational jobs** (`"be more productive"`): `[ANALYZE]` Symptom: verb is "be", no contextual clarifier. Fix: drill to a specific circumstance (`"reconcile invoices within 2 hours at month-end"`).
  2. **Solution-shaped jobs** (`"use a mobile app"`): `[ANALYZE]` Symptom: the verb or object names a solution. Fix: replace with the functional progress beneath (`"catch up on news while waiting in line"`).
  3. **Persona-driven jobs**: `[ANALYZE]` Symptom: jobs vary by persona label, not circumstance. Fix: re-cluster by circumstance, retire redundant persona bins.
  4. **Single-interview generalization**: `[EVALUATE]` Symptom: roadmap pivots on one switch story. Fix: minimum n=5 switch interviews per cluster before committing.
  5. **Ignoring Anxiety/Inertia**: `[EVALUATE]` Symptom: brilliant new feature ships, nobody switches. Fix: instrument onboarding to surface and reduce anxiety events before optimizing pull messages.
- **If Nothing Else, Remember This:** Most JTBD failures come from skipping the discipline — writing jobs from the conference-room whiteboard instead of from backward-walk interviews.
