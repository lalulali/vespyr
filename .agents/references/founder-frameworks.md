# Founder Frameworks Reference

Pick 2-4 frameworks based on the idea. Golden Circle + Pre-mortem are defaults — always run those two.

---

## Golden Circle (DEFAULT — always run)

**Use when:** Always. Tests if the idea has real purpose or is just a feature.

**Questions:**
- Why does this matter to a specific person? (not "everyone")
- What makes this approach meaningfully different? (10x, not 10%)
- Can you describe it to a stranger in 30 seconds?

**Red flag:** WHY is "because it's cool", "because AI exists", or "no one has done it" → kill or reframe.

**Output:** WHY: __ / HOW: __ / WHAT: __

---

## Pre-mortem (DEFAULT — always run)

**Use when:** Always. Forces concrete failure thinking before investment.

**Questions:**
- It's 12 months from now and this has failed. What killed it?
- Was it: wrong problem, wrong solution, wrong timing, wrong customer, wrong business model, execution failure, or market shift?
- What is the single biggest risk you are deliberately choosing to accept?

**Red flag:** You can't name the #1 risk in one sentence → you haven't thought hard enough.

**Output:** Failure scenario: __ / Root cause: __ / Accepted risk: __

---

## First Principles

**Use when:** Idea is buried under buzzwords, analogies, or "AI will solve it."

**Questions:**
- Strip away trends and jargon. What are the fundamental truths?
- What is the irreducible cost to deliver the value?
- What is the irreducible time for the user to get the benefit?

**Red flag:** You can't state the core mechanism in one sentence without jargon → you don't understand it yet.

**Output:** Core truth: __ / Irreducible cost: __ / Irreducible time: __

---

## Moat & Defensibility

**Use when:** Concept is feature-level, not platform-level — easy to copy.

**Questions:**
- Why couldn't a well-funded competitor copy this in 3 months?
- Is the advantage structural (network effects, data flywheel, switching costs) or superficial (feature gap, design polish)?
- What is the path to a durable advantage over 3-5 years?

**Red flag:** "We'll be first" or "we have better UX" is not a moat.

**Output:** Moat type: __ / Structural or superficial: __ / 3-year path: __

---

## Unit Economics

**Use when:** Revenue model is central to the idea (SaaS, marketplace, ads).

**Questions:**
- Roughly: what does it cost to acquire one user? What lifetime value does that user generate?
- Can you make a profit at 80% of your expected price? (stress-test pricing)
- What is the gross margin if you have to pay for infrastructure/APIs/integrations?

**Red flag:** The math only works at massive scale → you don't have a business, you have a lottery ticket.

**Output:** CAC: __ / LTV: __ / Margin at 80% price: __

---

## Market Timing

**Use when:** Idea depends on a recent shift (regulation, tech, culture).

**Questions:**
- Why does this idea make sense NOW and not 3 years ago or 3 years from now?
- What has changed in technology, regulation, culture, or behavior that unlocks this?
- Are you riding a wave or trying to create one? (Creating waves is expensive.)

**Red flag:** "The market isn't ready yet" usually means the idea isn't ready.

**Output:** Why now: __ / What changed: __ / Wave or create: __

---

## Dependency Stack

**Use when:** Idea requires external APIs, infrastructure, or user behavior change.

**Questions:**
- List everything that must already exist or be true for this to work (APIs, infrastructure, user behavior, regulations, hardware penetration).
- Which dependencies are "nice to have" vs "dealbreakers if missing"?
- How many of those dealbreakers are outside your control?

**Red flag:** The dependency stack is taller than the value proposition → the idea is fragile.

**Output:** Dealbreakers: __ / Outside control: __ / Stack vs value: __
